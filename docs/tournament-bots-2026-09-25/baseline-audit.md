# Ten baseline game reviews

Five Basic and five Riichi four-orbit games, selected in advance with seeds `tournament-audit:MODE:0..4`, at 24 samples. These use the previous policy adapted to the new Check/Call fishing rules. Every hand was summarized; these are diagnostic examples, not a claim that every losing move was a mistake. Complete alternatives, cards, and engine events are in the adjacent JSON files.

Across these ten games, **51 hands contained an all-in**: the first all-in was a Call in **38**, a Bet in **8**, and an ante payment in **5**. A hand can count as all-in even when nobody deliberately shoves. These small audit counts explain mechanisms; they are not balance estimates.

| Sample and trace | Decision reviewed | Assessment using information available at the time |
|---|---|---|
| [Basic 0](logs/old-basic.json.logs/0.md), H6 | P1 calls its last 45 into a displayed 250 pot, with 40% estimated equity and refund-adjusted 18% pot odds. | Defensible. Folding retains only nine antes. This call can be rational without validating the raises that created the short stack. |
| [Basic 1](logs/old-basic.json.logs/1.md), H4 | P1 shoves its last 150 at 74% estimated equity after six prior raises. P2 and P4 subsequently estimate 63% and 74% themselves. | Excessive confidence in a contested range. Different private information can justify different beliefs, but all players' optimistic estimates and seven Street-1 raises reveal insufficient conditioning on aggression. |
| [Basic 2](logs/old-basic.json.logs/2.md), H2 | P2 raises to 75 from a 140 stack, only five above the existing bet, at 52% estimated equity and 5% estimated whole-table foldout. P4 then shoves. | Poor early-tournament risk/reward. Calling could retain the free fish without reopening betting. Judge that alternative, not just whether the eventual hand won. |
| [Basic 3](logs/old-basic.json.logs/3.md), H3 | P4 and P2 each call their last five into a pot above 200, at 6% estimated equity and roughly 2% pot odds. | Definite errors found on card-level review: P3’s exposed cards already beat both complete hands, and fishing was locked. The uncertainty prior invented the quoted 6% chance from a known zero. Both calls should fold; the earlier 60-chip spending also deserves scrutiny. |
| [Basic 4](logs/old-basic.json.logs/4.md), H3 | P4 spends 25, then 115, then its final 65 on Street 1. P1 re-raises to 145 at 66% estimated equity. | The per-decision risk cap permits near-total exposure through repeated actions. Use a whole-hand budget and respect the stronger range implied by re-raises. |
| [Riichi 0](logs/old-riichi.json.logs/0.md), H9 | P2 calls, fishes the second Lotus, then shoves its remaining 305 with Twin Lotus. | Logical. Twin Lotus guarantees showdown victory; this all-in should remain available. P4 cannot see both concealed Lotuses, so losing its call alone does not establish irrationality. |
| [Riichi 1](logs/old-riichi.json.logs/1.md), H7 | P3 calls all 175 into a 480 pot at 48% estimated equity. Its utility is only about +4 despite +123 chip EV. | A genuinely marginal survival choice under its model. The old risk penalty nearly rejects it. Tournament position and a better opponent range can change the choice; stack size alone does not prove it wrong. |
| [Riichi 2](logs/old-riichi.json.logs/2.md), H5 | 23 bets/raises, many adding only five to a large wager while estimating double-digit fold chances. | A model-driven raise loop. Paying around 1% of the pot gives opponents little reason to fold. Later cheap calls can be sound while the preceding raises are not. |
| [Riichi 3](logs/old-riichi.json.logs/3.md), H1 | 21 bets/raises take the first pot to 515; several raises estimate only 2–3% whole-table foldout. | Poor escalation even without an all-in in this hand. It depletes stacks and primes later short-stack all-ins. All-in rate alone misses the behavior. |
| [Riichi 4](logs/old-riichi.json.logs/4.md), H12 | Two large stacks make 26 bets/raises in a 750-pot hand; minimum increments repeatedly receive 14–15% fold estimates. | Inflated fold equity. P2 has declared Riichi and cannot improve; P4 can fish when calling. Repeated raises need comparison with that alternative, not isolated chip-EV estimates. |

The selected generation's ten-game review is reported separately. Earlier candidate traces remain available for transparency and are not counted as independent validation.
