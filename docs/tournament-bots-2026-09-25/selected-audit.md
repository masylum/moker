# Ten selected-generation game reviews

The final frozen generation was replayed on the same five Basic and five Riichi seeds as the baseline audit, with four orbits and 24 samples. Every hand was reviewed through its action trace; the table highlights decisions that explain behavior. Results below are the **audit sample**, not the independent health cohort.

The revised Basic sample played 70 hands, reached Street 4 in 31, and had 13 all-in hands (six initiated by Calls, four by Bets, three by antes). The revised Riichi sample played 80 hands, reached Street 4 in 14, and had 50 all-in hands (36 Calls, seven Bets, seven antes). The old samples had 14/58 and 37/80 all-in hands respectively. Thus this audit does **not** show that the revision makes Riichi last longer. It shows shorter raise chains, improved certainty handling and different tournament incentives. The largest raise chain fell from 26 to eight in Riichi; Basic's maximum went from seven to eight.

All recorded decisions were checked for overlap between exposed cards and known concealed cards; none remained in the selected generation. Full JSON files next to each Markdown trace retain the observer's known cards, all evaluated alternatives, and engine events. Bets that exhaust a stack, Calls that exhaust a stack, and ante-triggered showdowns are kept distinct.

One remaining modeling weakness deserves a concrete example: in Riichi 0/H9, P4 calls its last 40 chips against a declared-Riichi shove while quoting 73% equity with two pairs. The opponent actually holds Twin Lotus, but that knowledge is not available to P4. The capped-pot price is 27%, so the choice follows the bot's estimate; the estimate itself looks optimistic against that action sequence. Public aggression now changes sampled ranges, but wager size and Riichi signals are not a fully calibrated opponent model. This is a concern, not a proof that the call was losing from the observer's information. Riichi 2/H10 contains a similar 76%-equity call with Three Winds against an unseen Twin Lotus shove.

These reviews demonstrate specific fixes and expose residual problems. They do not replace the independent head-to-head test, establish optimal play, or prove that every new decision dominates the old one.
